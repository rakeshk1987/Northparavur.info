# North Paravur – Where History, Heritage and Culture Meet

*A journey through history, from the ancient world of Muziris to modern Kerala.*

## 1. History – Ancient Paravur and Muziris

North Paravur is one of the historically significant regions of Kerala. Located in present-day Ernakulam district, close to the Periyar River, its tributaries and the surrounding backwaters, Paravur has been a meeting point of trade, culture and religious traditions since ancient times.

There are different explanations regarding the origin of the name Paravur. According to local traditions, the name may have evolved from 'Paravakkal Ur'. Another local interpretation suggests that it originated from 'Paraveyyur', meaning a coastal settlement. However, the exact origin of the name has not been conclusively established.

To understand the history of Paravur, it is important to explore the history of the ancient port of Muziris, located in the neighbouring region.

Muziris was one of the most important international trading centres of ancient Kerala. It is mentioned in Sangam literature and first-century Greek and Roman accounts. Located in the Periyar river region, the port was an important commercial centre associated with the early Chera kingdom.

Muziris maintained trading connections with regions including Egypt, the Roman Empire, the Greek world, Arabia and West Asia. The trade in spices, particularly black pepper, along with ivory, pearls and other valuable commodities, contributed to its importance. Foreign merchants arrived by sea and exchanged valuable goods, including gold, for Kerala's spices and other products.

Muziris represents the important role played by Kerala in the maritime trade networks connecting the ancient East and West.

## 2. Pattanam – Archaeological Remains of Ancient Muziris

One of the most important archaeological sites connected with the history of North Paravur is Pattanam.

Archaeological excavations began at Pattanam in 2007 under the leadership of the Kerala Council for Historical Research (KCHR). These excavations uncovered several findings that shed light on the international trading connections of ancient Kerala.

The discoveries include brick structures, ancient wells, storage jars, foreign pottery, objects associated with Roman and Mediterranean trade, and structures believed to be connected with ancient maritime activities.

The archaeological evidence suggests that Pattanam had settlement and trading activity from around the second century BCE to the tenth century CE.

Pattanam is widely considered a possible location of ancient Muziris or an important part of its trading settlement. However, the exact location of Muziris remains a subject of historical and archaeological discussion.

Nevertheless, the Pattanam excavations have significantly improved our understanding of Kerala's ancient international trade.

## 3. The Chera Dynasty and Mahodayapuram

The Cheras were among the most influential ruling dynasties in ancient South India. The early Cheras exercised considerable influence over parts of present-day Kerala and Tamil Nadu during the Sangam period.

Later, between approximately the ninth and twelfth centuries CE, the Chera Perumals ruled from Mahodayapuram, which became an important political, cultural and administrative centre.

Mahodayapuram was associated with present-day Kodungallur. The surrounding region, including the ancient port of Muziris, played a significant role in the political and commercial history of the Chera kingdom.

Paravur and its neighbouring settlements formed part of this wider historical and cultural landscape. Therefore, the history of Paravur is closely connected with the Cheras, Mahodayapuram and Muziris.

## 4. Christian Heritage – Kottakkavu Church

North Paravur has a significant connection with the early Christian traditions of Kerala through Kottakkavu Church.

According to Christian tradition, St. Thomas, one of the twelve apostles of Jesus Christ, arrived in Kerala in 52 CE and established Christian communities at several locations. Kottakkavu Church is traditionally recognised as one of the eight churches believed to have been founded by him.

An ancient Persian Cross preserved in the church premises is one of the important historical artefacts associated with the Christian heritage of the region. The cross is generally associated with around the ninth century and is considered evidence of the historical connections between Kerala's early Christian communities and the Persian Christian tradition.

Historical accounts also indicate that the church suffered damage during periods of Portuguese and Mysorean military activity and underwent reconstruction in later centuries.

## 5. Jewish Heritage – Paravur Synagogue

One of the most fascinating chapters in Kerala's history is the presence of Jewish communities along the Malabar Coast.

Jewish merchants arrived in Kerala during different periods of history and established communities in and around Kodungallur, Paravur and Kochi. They received certain privileges from local rulers and participated actively in regional trade and social life.

The Paravur Synagogue is an important reminder of this heritage.

The present building is believed to have been reconstructed around 1615–1616 on the site of an earlier synagogue traditionally dated to 1164.

The building is particularly interesting because it combines Jewish religious architecture with traditional Kerala architectural elements. Features such as the Padippura entrance, wooden decorations, prayer hall and special seating arrangements for women reflect this cultural combination.

The synagogue suffered damage during the Portuguese attack of 1635 and was subsequently rebuilt. It also suffered further damage during Tipu Sultan's military campaigns in the late eighteenth century.

By the middle of the twentieth century, a large proportion of Kerala's Jewish community had migrated to Israel, resulting in the decline of the Jewish community in Paravur.

Today, the building is preserved as the Kerala Jews History Museum under the Muziris Heritage Project. It stands as an important historical monument commemorating the life and culture of a once-active Jewish community in Kerala.

## 6. European Arrival and the Kingdom of Cochin

From the sixteenth century onwards, European powers began establishing a stronger presence along the Kerala coast.

The arrival of Vasco da Gama in 1498 marked an important turning point in Kerala's maritime history. The Portuguese gradually expanded their influence over coastal trade, particularly the lucrative spice trade.

The Dutch and later the English also became involved in the commercial and political affairs of the region.

The relationships and conflicts between the Kingdom of Cochin and European powers significantly influenced the history of central Kerala.

Paravur and its surrounding areas were also part of these political and commercial developments. The old fort of Paravur and local place names associated with fortifications preserve memories of earlier political arrangements.

According to the municipal historical account, the old mud fort of Paravur was destroyed during Tipu Sultan's attack on Travancore.

## 7. Chendamangalam and the Paliath Achans

One of the most historically significant places near North Paravur is Chendamangalam.

Chendamangalam was the ancestral home of the Paliath Achans, who served as the hereditary Prime Ministers of the Kingdom of Cochin. From approximately 1663 to 1809, the Paliath Achans exercised considerable influence over the political, administrative and military affairs of the kingdom.

The Paliam family maintained close relations with the Cochin royal family and played important roles in political negotiations and conflicts involving European powers.

Paliam Palace in Chendamangalam is an important monument that reflects the history and influence of this family. Another historic Paliam building, associated with the Dutch period, can also be found here.

Chendamangalam is particularly remarkable for another reason. A Jewish synagogue, churches, temples and historic Paliam buildings exist within the same geographical area.

This makes Chendamangalam one of the finest examples of Kerala's multicultural and religious heritage.

## 8. Travancore Rule and Nedumkotta

During the eighteenth century, the political landscape of Cochin, Travancore and Malabar underwent significant changes.

In 1762, following political and military developments, the King of Cochin ceded Paravur and Alangad to the Kingdom of Travancore, according to the historical records of Alangad Grama Panchayat.

As a result, Paravur became part of Travancore.

Meanwhile, Travancore developed an extensive defensive system to protect its northern territories against Mysorean invasions. One of the most important military fortifications was Nedumkotta, also known as the Travancore Lines.

The primary purpose of these fortifications was to defend Travancore against the military campaigns of Hyder Ali and later Tipu Sultan.

On 15 April 1790, Tipu Sultan's forces breached the Nedumkotta defensive line. The Mysorean army subsequently advanced through areas including Paravur and Alangad.

The military campaign severely affected the lives, agriculture and wealth of the local population. According to local accounts, many families abandoned their homes and possessions and fled to safer areas to escape the conflict.

## 9. The Freedom Movement and National Politics

Paravur also played a notable role in India's freedom movement and the democratic movements of Travancore.

According to the municipality's historical records, a local unit of the Travancore State Congress was established in Paravur in 1938. The region supported popular political movements demanding responsible government and greater democratic rights in Travancore.

Mahatma Gandhi visited Paravur and addressed a public gathering at the Paravur Concert Ground, according to the municipality's historical records.

Records also mention the Paravur Taluk Conference of the State Congress in 1945.

These events remain important parts of Paravur's political history and its contribution to the freedom struggle and democratic movements.

## 10. Modern Paravur – Municipality and Commerce

Over time, Paravur developed into an important commercial and administrative centre.

Established as a municipality in 1912, North Paravur is one of the older municipalities in Ernakulam district. Today, it serves as the headquarters of Paravur Taluk and as an important commercial and service centre for the surrounding villages.

In earlier times, one of the major traditional industries in Paravur was oil extraction using wooden presses. Agricultural products and local trade contributed significantly to the development of the regional economy.

The old Paravur market and its traditional commercial streets remain important reminders of the town's trading history.

## 11. India's First Experimental Use of Electronic Voting Machines

One of the most remarkable chapters in Paravur's modern history is its connection with the introduction of Electronic Voting Machines in India.

On 19 May 1982, during the Kerala Legislative Assembly election, Electronic Voting Machines were experimentally used at 50 out of 84 polling stations in the Paravur Assembly constituency.

This was the first experimental use of electronic voting machines in an Indian election.

However, the election result was challenged in court by A. C. Jose. The Supreme Court subsequently set aside the election because the election laws in force at the time did not provide the necessary statutory authority for the use of EVMs.

Legal amendments were later introduced to provide statutory recognition for electronic voting machines in Indian elections.

Today, EVMs are an integral part of India's electoral system. The fact that the first experimental use of this technology took place in the Paravur Assembly constituency remains a remarkable milestone in the modern history of the region.

## 12. Present-Day Paravur – A Living Heritage

Today, North Paravur continues to preserve its historical identity while developing as a modern town.

Its geographical landscape, shaped by the Periyar River, canals and backwaters, its historical monuments in Chendamangalam, archaeological discoveries at Pattanam, Kottakkavu Church and the Paravur Synagogue all contribute to its rich heritage.

A region associated with the ancient maritime trade of Muziris, part of the historical landscape of the Chera kingdom, home to early Christian and Jewish heritage, witness to the political changes between Cochin and Travancore, and the constituency where India's first experimental EVM voting took place – these are the many identities of Paravur.

A town carrying thousands of years of historical memories, North Paravur deserves a distinctive place in the history of Kerala.
